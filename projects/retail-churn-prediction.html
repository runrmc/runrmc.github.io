Project Overview

Customer retention is far cheaper than acquisition, but knowing who's actually at risk of leaving is non-trivial when a business has no explicit "churned" flag in its data. This project builds a full pipeline — from raw transactions to a deployable churn model and interactive dashboard — to identify at-risk customers and quantify the revenue tied to them.

The dataset is the UCI Online Retail II dataset: roughly 1.07 million transactions from a UK-based online gift retailer between December 2009 and December 2011, covering about 5,900 customers. It was deliberately chosen without a pre-built churn label, requiring the label itself to be engineered — a closer analog to real-world business problems than most tutorial datasets.

The Churn Labeling Problem

Roughly 243K rows missing a Customer ID were dropped during cleaning, and cancelled orders were split from purchases to preserve return behavior as a feature rather than discarding it. With no explicit churn flag, the label itself had to be engineered: I tested both 90-day and 180-day windows against a fixed reference date, and selected 180 days based on the dataset's observed ~180-day median purchase cycle — the 90-day window over-flagged normal infrequent buyers as "churned."

Features were built around RFM (recency, frequency, monetary) plus return rate, computed strictly on pre-reference-date data to avoid leakage. Pipeline logic was later refactored out of the notebook into reusable, type-hinted src/ modules, each independently runnable and testable.

Modeling & Results

A logistic regression baseline was followed by XGBoost, tuned via 5-fold grid search after an untuned version initially underperformed the baseline. Evaluation combined standard ML metrics (ROC-AUC, precision/recall) with a business-facing "revenue at risk" metric — since count-based recall alone doesn't capture which churners actually matter to the business.

  • Frequency, not recency, was the strongest churn predictor. Frequency (0.47 importance) outranked recency (0.28) and monetary value (0.23) — despite recency showing the most visually obvious relationship with churn during EDA.
  • The model systematically underperforms on high-value accounts. While the tuned model catches 77% of churners by count, it captures only 49.9% of the associated revenue. Missed churners average $2,163 in historical spend — over 3x the $649 average of correctly identified churners.
  • Reweighting the training objective closed part of that gap. Retraining XGBoost using monetary value as a sample weight improved revenue capture to 57.6% (+7.7 points), at a small cost to recall (0.77 → 0.75) and ROC-AUC (0.806 → 0.799) — confirming the gap was partly a training-objective issue, not purely a feature limitation. Model choice ultimately depends on whether the business cares more about reaching customers by count or protecting revenue.

The Dashboard

An interactive Streamlit dashboard lets you explore the project's results without running the notebook: toggle between the standard and value-weighted models to compare ROC-AUC, recall, and precision; view feature importance and RFM distributions by churn status; and look up any customer by ID to see their RFM profile alongside a live churn prediction and their actual historical outcome.
